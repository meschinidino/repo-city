The rules

  • Tiles are exact. Each generated tile's top face is measured (top, left and right corners), warped onto the exact grid diamond, and clipped to one shared tile shape. Neighboring tiles can't overlap or leave gaps.
  • Every building stands on a footprint plate. Buildings were generated on a flat cyan plate the size of their footprint (1×1, 2×1 or 2×2 tiles). The plate's corners set the building's scale and position, then the plate is keyed out. Anything that sticks out past the plate is counted.
  • Footprints never share a cell, and keep one cell of space. An occupancy grid places buildings on land only, landmarks near the island's center, the sea gate on a front-facing shore, everyone else spread out. Spacing is relaxed only if an island runs out of room, and the check says so.
  • Nothing hides much of anything else. Every building is rendered on its own and compared with what's drawn in front of it; the share of each building that stays hidden is reported.
  • Bridges are geometry. Decks run from the midpoint of one shore edge to the midpoint of the other, drawn in code in the sprites' wood colors.
  • Sprites are cut out as connected shapes, not by fixed grid cells, so a wide sprite can't be sliced.

Known gaps

  • Fixed in round 4: the market was regenerated (its plate now measures 1.95:1 and it's placed as 2×1; a 3×1 variant exists and would be picked automatically if its plate matched better), the flags were regenerated with their footings inside the plate, and trees are back as nine sprites on 1×1 plates, placed by their own rules.
  • Tile edges still wander up to 1.4% of a tile (about 3 px at 200 px per tile) because the generated tiles aren't perfectly straight. The fit hides it from overview; it's visible only if you look for it at full size.
  • Trees use 6 of the 9 types on this map, because the cell hash happens to land on them. A real map would pick types per island or by biome.
  • Checks cover geometry, not taste. They catch overlap, gaps, cropping and placement; whether it looks good is still your call.
